# Security tips

Amerox refuses the most dangerous requests and explains the rest, but most losses start with a person being talked into something. These habits close most of those doors.

## Your recovery phrase

- **Nobody legitimate will ever ask for it.** Not Amero X, not support, not a dapp, not an airdrop. Anyone who asks is trying to steal from you.
- Only ever type it inside the Amerox extension — when importing or recovering a wallet.
- Keep it on paper, offline. Never in photos, email, chat or cloud notes.

## Before you sign

- Check the site's address in the approval window. Phishing sites copy real ones and change a letter.
- Read **what** you're approving: the amount, the recipient, and whether it's a permission to spend.
- Be very careful with **unlimited** approvals and permits. Approve only the amount you're trading when you can.
- When in doubt, reject. You can always try again.

## Before you send

- Copy addresses from the source, not from your transaction history — "address poisoning" scams rely on look-alike addresses in your history. Amerox warns you about look-alikes, but check anyway.
- Confirm the network with the person you're paying.
- Send a small test amount first for large transfers.

## Your device

- Use a strong, unique password for Amerox. It protects the wallet on this device.
- Set **Settings → Auto-lock** to a short time, and lock the wallet when you step away.
- Install browser extensions only from sources you trust. A malicious extension on the same browser is a risk to every wallet.
- Keep your browser and operating system up to date.

## Airdrops and "free" tokens

Unknown tokens that appear in your wallet are often bait. Don't try to sell or approve them on a site you don't know — the site is the trap, not the token.
